How can a lender cut credit losses without reviewing every application?

Ranking 307,511 loan applicants by default risk to focus a lender's review effort.

A lender cannot review every application in depth. The question was where review effort should go, and which model should be trusted to send it there.

The short answer

Rank every applicant by risk and send only the riskiest fifth to a person. That one change catches 53% of the loans that go bad.

  • Decide with a model you can explain. A transparent scorecard comes within 0.012 AUC of the best machine-learning model, and every decision has a reason.
  • Set the cut-off from your own economics. The software default flags almost no one. The right threshold comes from your margins and loss rates.
  • Remove gender before deployment. It adds almost nothing to accuracy, and it should not be in a credit decision.

The approach

We compared six models on 307,511 past applicants, from the industry-standard logistic regression to XGBoost, then tested whether an explainable banded scorecard could close the gap. A fifth of applicants were held back and used only once, for the final comparison.

What it means

Review the riskiest fifth and catch over half the problem loans. About 21% of the applicants flagged at that setting go on to default, 2.6 times the average. The usual 50% probability cut-off flags almost no one: only 201 applicants, catching 106 of 4,965 defaults.

What we recommended

  1. Use the explainable banded scorecard as the decision model, and send the riskiest 20% to manual review.
  2. Run XGBoost alongside it as a challenger, and switch only if the extra defaults it catches outweigh the cost of governing a less transparent model.
  3. Set the review cut-off from the lender's margin and loss figures, not the default 50%.
  4. Remove gender before deployment (AUC moves from 0.7657 to 0.7644), audit approval rates across groups, and review the use of age with legal counsel.

Limits

Results use the main application table only, from one time period. Flagged applicants should be reviewed, not declined automatically: even the best model flags about 3.7 good borrowers for each defaulter it catches.
