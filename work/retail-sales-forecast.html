Why does the same promotion earn twice as much on a Monday?

Forecasting daily sales for 100 drugstores to plan promotions, inventory and staffing.

Stock and staff are scheduled store by store, day by day. One chain-wide forecast hides exactly the differences that matter.

The short answer

Because stores and weekdays respond differently. Plan stock, staff and promotions store by store and day by day, and the forecast stays inside its stated range 95% of the time.

  • Promote early in the week. In the same store, a Monday promotion lifts sales about twice as much as a Friday one.
  • Forecast each store on its own. Treating stores individually was the single biggest gain in accuracy.
  • Judge promotions on profit next. The next step is adding price, discount and margin data, so the question becomes what a promotion earns, not just what it sells.

The approach

Using only what is known before the day starts (the calendar, the store's profile and the promotion schedule), we modelled daily sales for 100 stores. 2014 was used to build the model and January to February 2015 to test it.

What it means

Promotional stock and staffing should follow the store and the day. A Monday promotion in a format A store lifts sales more than twice as much as a Friday promotion in a format C store. One chain-wide assumption would overstock some stores and leave others short.

What we recommended

  1. Plan inventory and staffing from store-specific forecasts, using the planning ranges for safety stock.
  2. Schedule promotions where the lift is highest, and staff for it.
  3. Add pricing, discount depth, promotion cost and margin data next, to move from predicting demand to managing it.

Limits

The model has no price, cost or stock-out data, so it cannot say whether a promotion was profitable. It covers 100 of the chain's 1,115 stores.
